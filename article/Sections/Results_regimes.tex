\subsection{\blt{Other Regimes and the Live Test}} \label{s:regimes}\label{s:live}
The 2023--2025 window is a bull market. Table~\ref{tab:regimes} repeats the comparison on three further 500-day windows with the same universe, protocol and hyperparameters and 100 portfolios each: 2019--2020 with the COVID crash, 2021--2022 with the bear market, and September 2024 to September 2026, which lies after the data used to design and tune the method. The first two use the same 20 stocks before the 2021 screen that selected them and therefore carry its bias; the third does not.

Three facts hold in every window. RA stays below the rebalanced equal-weight portfolio in Sharpe ratio ($-0.2$ to $-0.8$, $p<0.001$), paying $11$--$15\%$ of its wealth in costs. The legacy heuristic is the equal-weight portfolio plus one percentage point of daily turnover and $0.02$ below it in Sharpe ratio. Against mean-variance the comparison reverses with the regime: mean-variance made $4$--$7\times$ in 2019--2020 and lost money in 2021--2022, whereas RA has the smaller drawdown in $76$--$96\%$ of the portfolios everywhere and the higher Sharpe ratio in the bear market ($+0.78$). In 2021--2022 both versions of the policy also beat the index, as does every low-turnover strategy on this universe; in the other windows the index wins.

\input{Tables/regimes}

The live test of 1 March to 15 April 2026 (Fig.~\ref{fig:cum_wealth_live}, Table~\ref{tab:live_metrics}) returned $2.3\%$ against $2.2\%$ for the S\&P~500 and $5.6\%$ for the equal-weight portfolio, with the largest drawdown of the three. We keep it as a demonstration that the pipeline runs end-to-end on a broker feed, not as evidence of performance: 32 trading days cannot separate strategies whose daily returns differ by a fraction of a percent, and the 2024--2026 window, which contains those dates, gives the same ranking on 100 portfolios.
