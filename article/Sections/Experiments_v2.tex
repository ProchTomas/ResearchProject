\section{Experimental Results}
\label{s:experiments}
This section compares the proposed policy with standard benchmarks on paired portfolios, reports what each component contributes, and tests the adaptive forgetting. The protocol, the paired tests, the full ablation and sensitivity tables, the stress tests, three further market windows and the live test are in the complementary material, referred to below as Secs.~S1--S11; the thesis \cite{Pro:26} reports further variants. All numbers come from the released code (Sec.~\ref{s:software}), which regenerates them from a single script.

\subsection{\blt{Setup}} \label{s:data}
From the S\&P~500 constituents we took the 100 stocks with the highest average daily return in 2021 and, among them, the 20 least pair-wise correlated ones. We draw 200 portfolios of 8--12 stocks at random with a fixed seed and run every strategy, benchmark and ablation on exactly the same portfolios and days, so that all comparisons are paired. Each portfolio uses 750 trading days, 4 November 2022 -- 31 October 2025: 125 days build the prior (Sec.~\ref{s:prior}), 125 days feed the structure estimation (Sec.~\ref{s:SE}), and the remaining 500 days are the test period. Every strategy starts from the uniform allocation and pays $0.2\%$ of the value actually traded, the drift of the weights caused by the realised returns excluded. Differences across portfolios are tested with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, a bootstrap $95\%$ confidence interval of the mean difference and the win rate. The hyperparameters $\mu,\alpha_0,\beta_0$ were fixed by a $2^k$ design \cite{4736103} on 2021--2022 data, disjoint from the test period. Secs.~S1--S3 give the universe, the dates, the seeds, the cost model, the metrics and every benchmark in full, Sec.~S4 the end-to-end algorithm with all user-set quantities and their origin.

\subsection{\blt{Results on the 2023--2025 Test Period}} \label{s:results}
Table~\ref{tab:bench_mean} reports the means over the 200 portfolios; the paired tests and the median wealth paths are in Sec.~S3.

With the corrected optimiser the Risk-Averse policy (RA) reaches an annualised Sharpe ratio of $0.34$ and a total return of $1.11$, against $0.66$ and $1.27$ for the daily rebalanced equal-weight portfolio ($\Delta$Sharpe $-0.32$, $p<0.001$, RA better in $26\%$ of the portfolios). The cause is in the last column: RA trades $15\%$ of the portfolio per day and pays $15\%$ of the initial wealth in costs over the two years, ten times the equal-weight portfolio, while before costs the two are comparable. On risk it does what its name promises: its maximum drawdown $-25.5\%$ is smaller than that of both equal-weight portfolios and far smaller than that of every mean-variance variant ($-40\%$ to $-42\%$, $p<0.001$), and only the 250-day minimum-variance portfolio is better.

The turnover has a single cause. The risk weight $\omega^{\ast}$ of Sec.~\ref{s:FPD} is returned as the upper end $\omega_{\max}=10^3$ of the searched interval in all $10^5$ decision steps, because the necessary condition for an extreme is a sum of terms that are positive for $\mat{D}\succ\mat{O}$, $\hat{\mat{\Sigma}}\succeq\mat{O}$, $\omega>0$ and therefore has no root. At $\omega=10^3$ the variance term of \eqref{e:lossmatrixQdefinition} exceeds the transaction-cost term by four orders of magnitude, so the cost effectively leaves the loss and the allocation is re-optimised from scratch every day. This is also the mechanism behind the small drawdowns.

The legacy softmax policy, which produced the tables of the previous version, keeps the weights within a daily turnover of $2.9\%$ of uniform and is then indistinguishable from the rebalanced equal-weight portfolio in return and $0.03$ below it in Sharpe ratio ($p<0.001$). Our earlier claim of outperforming the uniform portfolio rested on charging that portfolio no costs and does not survive a paired test. The S\&P~500 returned $1.62$ at a Sharpe ratio of $1.58$ over these days, which no strategy on this 20-stock universe approaches, equal-weight buy-and-hold included; we therefore make no claim against the index either.

\subsection{\blt{What the Components Contribute}} \label{s:ablation_results}\label{s:sensitivity}\label{s:stress}
Each component was removed or replaced in turn on 100 of the portfolios and the user-set quantities were varied around their values; Secs.~S5--S7 give the tables.

Three components are indispensable, and for the same reason: dropping the FPD risk term ($-72\%$ of the capital), sampling $\theta$ instead of using the point estimate ($-56\%$) and omitting the structure estimation ($-49\%$) all widen or distort the predictive density that the loss then takes at face value. Three are inert: the corrected constrained solver and an independent SLSQP reference agree to $3\cdot10^{-7}$ in the allocation, the horizon $h=5$ reproduces $h=1$ to $7\cdot10^{-6}$, and replacing the data-based forgetting by a plain Bayesian update changes no metric measurably (Sharpe $+0.04$, $p=0.13$), because the optimised weight of the prior stays between $0.5$ and $0.8$ on almost every day. Of the tuned quantities only the prior shrinkage $\mu$ matters, and $0.9$ is not its best value: $\mu=0.7$ keeps 15 of 137 regressors instead of 57 and gains $0.18$ in Sharpe ratio ($p<0.001$), still below the equal-weight portfolio. One ablation is a trap: with a flat prior the forecasts vanish and the constrained minimiser is exactly $1/N$, so the apparently best variant (Sharpe $0.72$) is the equal-weight portfolio under another name.

Capping the interval of $\omega$ shows that no value of it rescues the design: at $\omega_{\max}=1$ the policy reproduces the rebalanced equal-weight portfolio, and at $\omega_{\max}=10$ or $100$, as well as for three principled elicitations, the forecasts dominate the variance term and $46$--$73\%$ of the capital is lost. The reason is the forecast itself, which over the test period has the same standard deviation as the realised returns ($0.027$), a correlation of $-0.01$ with them, a sign accuracy of $49.7\%$ and twice the squared error of predicting zero. A loss that takes it at face value trades on noise, so the point forecast has to be shrunk, or the posterior uncertainty integrated into the design, before it enters the loss (Sec.~S7).

On synthetic ARX data with known parameters, one-day crashes on all assets and an abrupt change of all coefficients (Sec.~S8), the forgetting sets $\beta=0$ and $\gamma\approx1$ on the crash days: the record is discarded, the statistic reverts to the prior and the estimation error does not move, while a plain Bayesian update and exponential forgetting both absorb the outlier. This is the robustness claimed in Sec.~\ref{s:forgetting}; its price is that the mechanism protects by not learning, so the model does not re-learn after the parameter change either, which is why removing the forgetting costs nothing in the ablation. Repeating the comparison on the COVID crash, the 2022 bear market, a window posterior to all data used to design the method, and a 32-day live test on a broker feed (Secs.~S9 and~S10) leaves the ranking against the equal-weight portfolio and the smaller drawdown against mean-variance, which holds in $76$--$96\%$ of the portfolios, unchanged in every window.
